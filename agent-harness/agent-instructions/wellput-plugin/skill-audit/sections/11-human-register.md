# Human register: sentence audit

Sentence IDs S55-S60. [Audit overview](../../skill-audit.md). [Full sentence review](../../skill-review.md#human-register).

## Section assessment

Keep an adaptive positive register and the permission to preserve legitimate voice, precision, and nuance. Do not turn
the section into a universal ban on all headings, contrasts, triples, or unfamiliar technical language. Several
negatives name real failures, but others rely on undefined criteria such as 'several signals' or duplicate mechanical
checks that ignore exact user requests.

Most useful content: the audience principle in S55 and the positive objective in S56. Main repair: preserve intentional
structure, narrow actual filler, and move fixed audience assumptions out of the portable default. See F01, F05, and F07.

## S55: Keep and reconcile portability

> Match demonstrated expertise.

**Assessment:** Matching demonstrated expertise is a meaningful adaptive principle and should not be dismissed for being
judgment. The public injected policy contradicts that adaptability when it fixes every user as a senior engineer and
directs the agent to skip remedial explanation.

**Related:** S56 defines register, S37 explains unfamiliar terms, and the exported interactive peer-communication
section supplies a fixed audience assumption.

**Recommendation:** Keep expertise calibration in the portable skill. Make any fixed senior-engineer relationship a
consumer-specific preference rather than a universal plugin default.

**Principles:** Audience fit; scope and portability.

## S56: Keep a smaller positive target

> Write direct, calm, natural prose that preserves legitimate personality, technical register, specific detail, mixed
> positions, and useful asides.

**Assessment:** This preserves personality, technical detail, and nuance, which are important counterweights to
mechanical concision. The long adjective inventory is difficult to evaluate as a set of independent requirements.
'Legitimate' personality and 'useful' asides need the reader's task as their criterion.

**Related:** S55 calibrates expertise, S57 removes filler, S12 protects necessary technical terms, and S24 limits
redundant explanation.

**Recommendation:** Keep a concise positive objective: write direct, natural prose with the precision and context the
reader needs; retain voice and nuance that support meaning.

**Principles:** Positive guidance; density; useful judgment.

## S57: Consolidate without losing the failure

> Remove canned reactions, unneeded self-announcements, obvious headings, praise, unneeded offers, promotional
> language, inflated significance, vague authority, slogans, and conclusions that only repeat the opening.

**Assessment:** The inventory mixes clear failure classes with subjective judgments such as 'obvious' headings and
'unneeded' offers. A requested heading is not filler. Reaction and narration openers are partly enforced mechanically,
but those checks lack a user-request exception and reject an exact requested word.

**Related:** S11 protects requested artifacts, S59 narrows ease claims, S60 governs style rewriting, and the
exact-'Absolutely' probe demonstrates the mechanical boundary problem.

**Recommendation:** State the positive behavior and a few named traps: start with substance and remove repetition or
reactions that add no information; retain requested structure and exact text.

**Principles:** Positive guidance; named failures; task precedence.

## S58: Keep as a conditional anti-padding rule

> Do not force groups of three, manufacture a `not X but Y` opposition, or invent a range without a scale.

**Assessment:** The word 'force' matters: the rule rejects invented content to complete a rhetorical pattern, not every
group of three. Therefore it does not inherently conflict with three required handoff labels. The `not X but Y`
prohibition can still suppress a requested contrast or a distinction that carries real meaning.

**Related:** S18 can legitimately choose a before-and-after contrast; S13 forbids invention or loss of facts; configured
labels are a separate intentional format.

**Recommendation:** Do not invent a third point, a contrast, or a numeric range for rhetorical symmetry. Preserve a
requested or meaning-bearing structure.

**Principles:** Exception clarity; avoid false contradictions.

## S59: Merge with evidence and tone

> Call work easy, simple, obvious, or quick only when that fact changes the reader's action or expectation.

**Assessment:** This is a useful curb on empty reassurance, but whether the fact changes 'action or expectation' is
itself subjective. A supported statement about low implementation effort can matter; the real trap is an ungrounded
promise or a claim that makes the reader feel dismissed.

**Related:** S57 already removes filler, S26 separates evidence from judgment, and S56 preserves an appropriate human
register.

**Recommendation:** Use concrete constraints or evidence when an effort or difficulty claim matters. Remove unsupported
'easy' or 'obvious' reassurance.

**Principles:** Evidence; named failure; density.

## S60: Replace the undefined trigger

> Rewrite machine-like wording only when several signals combine, and preserve every fact the changed wording carried.

**Assessment:** 'Several signals combine' has no defined signals or threshold, so it cannot guide a consistent revision.
'Machine-like' also risks treating any unfamiliar but correct technical wording as a defect. The preservation clause is
useful but already appears in fidelity.

**Related:** S12 and S39 protect legitimate terms; S32 and S62 distinguish word substitution from actual meaning repair;
S56 supplies the positive style target.

**Recommendation:** Rewrite wording that obscures the actor, action, relationship, or intended register; preserve facts
and exact technical literals. Do not require an undefined signal count.

**Principles:** Trigger clarity; observable repair.
