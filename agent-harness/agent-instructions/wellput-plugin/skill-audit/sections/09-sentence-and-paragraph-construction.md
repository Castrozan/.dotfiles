# Sentence and paragraph construction: sentence audit

Sentence IDs S40-S47. [Audit overview](../../skill-audit.md). [Full sentence review](../../skill-review.md#sentence-and-paragraph-construction).

## Section assessment

This section mixes a concrete observed failure, opaque noun stacks, with ordinary grammar preferences and an undefined
punctuation objective. The generic rules may be useful for telegraphic budget-constrained output, but the audit found no
sentence-specific experiment proving their individual value. Prefer the narrow relationship failures over blanket
abbreviation, active-voice, or one-action counting rules.

Most useful content: S41 and S46, with conditions. Main repair: make style adaptive to task and audience and reduce
generic grammar instructions only through controlled comparisons. See F07 and F09.

## S40: Make conditional

> Introduce one stable short form after a long technical name.

**Assessment:** The sentence requires a short form merely because a technical name is long, even when it appears once.
It can add an invented abbreviation and another mapping the reader must remember. That undermines the stable established
vocabulary the adjacent sections protect.

**Related:** S10 discourages unnecessary new terms, S38 requires stability, and S55 asks for audience calibration.

**Recommendation:** Introduce a conventional short form only when the term recurs and the abbreviation reduces reader
effort; define it once.

**Principles:** Proportionality; terminology consistency.

## S41: Keep but target ambiguity

> Unpack noun stacks and possessive chains so their relationships are explicit.

**Assessment:** Unpacking a genuinely opaque noun stack is a useful response to a documented failure. Applying it to
every noun compound or possessive chain would expand familiar domain expressions for no gain. The existing
opaque-noun-stack case is a better criterion than the grammatical form alone.

**Related:** S14 resolves the relationship; S37 preserves necessary technical terms; S47 targets the same reading load
at punctuation level.

**Recommendation:** Unpack a noun stack when it hides who acts, what changes, or which thing depends on another. Leave
familiar precise compounds intact.

**Principles:** Named failure; source fidelity.

## S42: Make a default rather than a closed list

> Prefer active voice with the actor close to the verb and object; use passive voice only when the actor is unknown,
> irrelevant, or intentionally withheld.

**Assessment:** The active-voice preference is comprehensible, but the passive-voice exceptions are framed as
exhaustive. An artifact-focused sentence can legitimately name a known, relevant actor in a passive construction, so the
actor need not be unknown, irrelevant, or withheld. Requested quotations and writing styles must also retain their own
wording. This is a scope concern, not a measured model failure.

**Related:** S11 controls requested transformations and quotations; S46 asks for the paragraph's main point first; S56
preserves legitimate register.

**Recommendation:** Prefer active voice when it clarifies the actor or action. Keep passive constructions when they
better serve the requested focus or source fidelity.

**Principles:** Conditional style; task precedence.

## S43: Reduce to the observed failure

> Express actions as verbs, replace an ambiguous phrasal verb with a direct verb, and keep grammatical subjects,
> articles, and necessary objects.

**Assessment:** This bundles several elementary grammar rules with the more useful requirement to express actions
directly. It is a strong candidate for reducing generic instruction load unless telegraphic phrases and missing subjects
are recurring measured failures. No removal experiment was run, so the benefit of deleting it remains a hypothesis.

**Related:** S41 and S47 cover relationship clarity; S56 covers natural prose; S61 already includes a revision step for
sentences.

**Recommendation:** Keep an operational condition such as 'State who does what; replace a vague verb phrase when it
hides the action.' Preserve literal source text.

**Principles:** Density; one concern; failure-based guidance.

## S44: Relax the atomization rule

> Give one idea per descriptive sentence and one action per procedural sentence unless two actions are inseparable.

**Assessment:** One action per procedural sentence is a useful default but 'unless inseparable' is a stricter exception
than readability requires. Two separable, closely related UI actions can fit naturally in one short step, while one
action with several hidden prerequisites can still be difficult to follow.

**Related:** S46 defines paragraph purpose, S48 defines operational order, and S63 checks whether a procedure works as
written.

**Recommendation:** Split a sentence or step when combining actions hides their order, dependency, or failure condition,
rather than counting actions mechanically.

**Principles:** Outcome-based style; procedural clarity.

## S45: Narrow

> Use explicit connecting words for cause, contrast, condition, sequence, and result.

**Assessment:** Explicit connectives can expose a consequential cause or condition. Requiring a connecting word for
every relationship can create mechanical prose and restate relationships already clear from the grammar or visual. The
issue is whether the relationship is missing, not whether a particular grammatical marker appears.

**Related:** S14 and S41 expose the underlying relationship; S24 avoids duplicating a visual; S47 also targets the
reader's working load.

**Recommendation:** Make decision-changing causes, conditions, and ordering explicit, using a connective only where it
adds that information.

**Principles:** Density; semantic versus syntactic control.

## S46: Keep as a default

> Give each paragraph one reader need and put its main point first.

**Assessment:** Organizing a paragraph around one reader need and placing its main point first is useful, testable at
review time, and aligned with answer-first communication. It should remain a drafting heuristic rather than a new fixed
schema that forbids a necessary qualification before a conclusion.

**Related:** S1 establishes the need, S27 leads with a recommendation, and S53 controls an explanation's progression.

**Recommendation:** Keep the principle near the reader contract; avoid repeating answer-first wording in every section.

**Principles:** Cohesion; canonical ownership.

## S47: Replace vague optimization

> Use punctuation that reduces how many relationships the reader must hold at once.

**Assessment:** The phrase 'how many relationships the reader must hold at once' has no defined operational measure. It
risks treating punctuation as a proxy for understanding when the real problem is unnamed actors, nested conditions, or
sentence structure. This is judgment, not a mechanically verifiable action despite the earlier Action label.

**Related:** S41 addresses opaque relationship packing, S44 addresses clause load, and S62 allows restructuring when
wording alone fails.

**Recommendation:** Split nested conditions or clauses when punctuation cannot make the actor, dependency, and result
clear.

**Principles:** Observable action; avoid proxy metrics.
