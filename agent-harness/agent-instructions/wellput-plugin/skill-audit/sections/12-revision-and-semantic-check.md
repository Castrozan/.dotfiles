# Revision and semantic check: sentence audit

Sentence IDs S61-S63. [Audit overview](../../skill-audit.md). [Full sentence review](../../skill-review.md#revision-and-semantic-check).

## Section assessment

An ordered revision pass adds a workflow that is not identical to a bag of style rules. Its current sentence copies most
of the earlier inventories and becomes a second owner for them. Keep the order and point to canonical checks. A
self-review is a drafting operation, not proof of factual accuracy or evidence of improved model adherence.

Most useful content: the pass itself and the procedure completeness check. Main repair: compress repetition and
distinguish textual walkthrough from authorized operational execution. See F06, F07, and F09.

## S61: Keep the workflow, remove copied inventories

> Revise in this order: 1) verify facts, reasoning, and the requested task by matching every material quantity with
> its unit and bound, condition, named artifact, and uncertainty from the source to the draft; 2) confirm that the
> reader can recover the answer, actor, action, evidence, conditions, limits, and next step; 3) match the draft's
> format to `[representation selection](#representation-selection)` and order the information; 4) standardize terms
> and expose hidden relationships; 5) tighten sentences; 6) scan for ambiguity, unsupported certainty, formulaic
> voice, and channel constraints.

**Assessment:** The ordered revision pass adds a distinct workflow and is worth keeping. Most of its content restates
S3, S9-S15, S18, S38, and S57, so it becomes a second prose owner for their wording. It should not be interpreted as a
claim that a self-check proves correctness or replaces external validation.

**Related:** S1 supplies the task, fidelity owns facts, representation owns form, and register owns filler. Tests
currently pin the section name even if its content is consolidated.

**Recommendation:** Use a short ordered pass that points to the canonical checks: verify source meaning, confirm the
answer, choose the form, then remove avoidable reading work.

**Principles:** Canonical ownership; density; evaluation boundary.

## S62: Merge with recovery or keep as a narrow fallback

> Rewrite sentence structure when word substitution cannot restore meaning.

**Assessment:** Changing sentence structure rather than swapping words is a useful fallback for a genuinely unclear
relationship. It is the actionable version of much of S32-S33's explanatory material. Repeating it in both recovery and
revision increases the number of places that can drift.

**Related:** S30 identifies the missing relationship, S32 warns about superficial substitution, and S47 targets
overloaded clauses.

**Recommendation:** Keep one fallback: restructure the explanation when the relationship is still unclear after
terminology is resolved.

**Principles:** Single ownership; observable repair.

## S63: Disambiguate the walkthrough

> Before returning a procedure, follow only its actions in order and check `[prerequisites and
hazards](#procedures-and-explanations)` at their first affected action.

**Assessment:** Checking a procedure using only its stated actions is a strong completeness test. 'Follow only its
actions' can mean either mentally walking through the instructions or actually executing them. A writing skill must not
silently authorize executing an operational or destructive procedure merely to validate its prose.

**Related:** S48-S51 define the procedure's content and order; Core authorization and Completion govern actual execution
and verification.

**Recommendation:** Read through the procedure as its intended reader, checking that each prerequisite, input, and
hazard is available before the affected step. Execute only under the task's existing authority.

**Principles:** Scope; authorization boundary; clear operation.
