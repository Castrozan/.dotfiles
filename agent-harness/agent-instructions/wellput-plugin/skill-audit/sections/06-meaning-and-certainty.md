# Meaning and certainty: sentence audit

Sentence IDs S25-S28. [Audit overview](../../skill-audit.md). [Full sentence review](../../skill-review.md#meaning-and-certainty).

## Section assessment

The distinction between observed facts, inferences, and proposals is part of good communication and should remain.
Factual verification, authorization, and operational completion are separate authorities. The local pointer to Core
makes architectural sense in dotfiles; copying broad Core sections into a portable writing plugin expands the dependency
beyond this section's job. The audit flags that packaging scope rather than recommending that uncertainty communication
disappear.

Most useful content: S26-S28. Main repair: consolidate missing-evidence language and narrow the portable epistemic
dependency. See F04 and F05.

## S25: Move to a narrow boundary

> Core `[evidence](references/core.md#evidence)` owns epistemic judgment.

**Assessment:** Pointing to the epistemic owner is good local architecture, but the exported owner is a copy of broad
Evidence, Autonomy, and Completion rules. Those include implementation choices, testing, CI watching, and workspace
stewardship, which exceed a portable writing skill's responsibility. The sentence itself gives the writer no operation
to perform.

**Related:** S15, S26, and S28 own how uncertainty is communicated. The exporter selects entire core sections; injected
peer and completion rules refer to them.

**Recommendation:** Retain a concise boundary to truthfulness and evidence. Audit the exported dependency separately
instead of importing unrelated operational policy as the reader's 'Core'.

**Principles:** Scope and horizon; portable dependencies.

## S26: Keep and clarify

> In human-facing output, keep observation, source evidence, inference, assumption, recommendation, and decision
> distinct when the difference changes confidence or action.

**Assessment:** Separating observed facts from inferences and decisions is a strong semantic requirement. 'Keep ...
distinct' does not specify whether every answer needs six headings or annotations; that would burden simple replies and
fragment normal reasoning. The condition about changed confidence or action is valuable.

**Related:** S15 handles unknowns, S28 names supporting evidence, and Core Evidence owns factual verification. S61
should reference this check instead of restating its inventory.

**Recommendation:** When the distinction matters, say what is observed, what is inferred, and what is proposed, using
ordinary connective language rather than a mandatory taxonomy.

**Principles:** Observable meaning; proportionality.

## S27: Keep and give precedence

> Lead an action recommendation with the action.

**Assessment:** This is one of the most concise actionable rules and directly supports the decision-first evaluation. It
needs no extra explanation in the skill. Its current collision is with visual-first ordering, not with the usefulness of
diagrams.

**Related:** S1 establishes the requested decision, S20 controls visual order, and S53 controls explanatory order.

**Recommendation:** Keep this as the default for recommendations and resolve visual placement around it. Put the chosen
action where the reader encounters it first.

**Principles:** Answer-first ordering; arbitration.

## S28: Merge with evidence communication

> State the evidence for a diagnosis or recommendation, including observed state, scope, and timing, and name missing
> evidence that limits it.

**Assessment:** This preserves the evidence and limits behind a diagnosis, but repeats the missing-fact behavior of S15.
'Observed state, scope, and timing' is useful when those details change the claim, not a required three-field schema for
every recommendation.

**Related:** S15 is the uncertainty owner; S26 separates evidence from inference; S3 and S61 repeat parts of the same
inventory.

**Recommendation:** Use one evidence paragraph that states the support and decision-changing limits, with state, scope,
and time included when material.

**Principles:** Density; conditional scope.
