## What it actually is

Two related monochrome pieces, white on pure black, built out of box-drawing and block characters.

- The quoted clip is a dim regular glyph lattice filling the frame with a bright horizontal stratum of blocks
  and line-work moving through it. The bright band reads as a playhead over a static score.
- The captured clip is the same vocabulary as a **plotter**: a bright emission front sits near the top and
  structure accretes downward from it, dimming with age until the oldest rows are a sparse dot field. Long
  horizontal runs, vertical traces that jog sideways through rounded corners, and solid or dithered blocks
  hanging off the traces. It reads as a circuit schematic printing itself.

The audio is broadband noise band-limited at about 15.3kHz with its energy under 2kHz, swelling and thinning
against the visual density rather than playing a tune. This is sonification, image to sound, and the caption
flags it as the point ("sound on ;)").

Two things bounded the verdict rather than the aesthetics.

**The sound cannot ship.** The player hard-mutes at
`ambient-canvas/swift-sources/ambient-canvas-recorded-loop-video-view.swift:24`
(`recordedLoopQueuePlayer.isMuted = true`), and `bad-apple/seekable_video_source.js:78` mutes its video too.
A screensaver running 24/7 is silent by design and should stay that way, so the adopt keeps the score and
drops the sonification. That is the honest cost and it is half of what the artist was pointing at.

**The repo has the inverse machine, and it is the wrong fit.** `.config/quickshell/bar/dashboard/services/
CavaService.qml` feeds a 24-bar visualiser at `.../tabs/MediaTab.qml:84-108` on chise, with real audio. But
that runs sound to image while this piece runs image to sound, so dressing cava's bars in box-drawing glyphs
would borrow the vocabulary and drop the technique, and it would make a working everyday widget busier for
no gain. Rejected in favour of the screensaver.

It is also genuinely distinct from what is already there. `matrix_rain.js` is independently falling columns
of random katakana in green with a fade trail; nothing in it is structural or connected. `bad-apple/` is
braille downsampling of video luminance. This is white-on-black connected line-work with a coherent
emission front, and the whole field is a pure function of the clock rather than accumulated state.

## Reasoning

Faithful where the medium allows and reinterpreted where it does not. The source is portrait 6:7 and the
loop is a fixed 1920x1080, and the clip is 15s of a piece that starts empty and fills, so the scene renders
the steady state at the loop's own resolution with 512 rows of pre-roll: at `localElapsedSeconds` 0 the field
is already full, which a straight transcription would not give.

The model is deterministic by construction. Every trace segment, jog, component bar, bus and lattice dot is
an integer hash of `(traceIndex, segmentIndex)` or `(column, sourceRow)`, with no `Math.random`, no
`performance.now`, no `Date` and no accumulated state, so `render(t)` is a pure function and the recorder
reproduces it frame for frame (`README.md:107`). Because it is a 2D canvas scene rather than WebGL, the
`preserveDrawingBuffer` trap at `README.md:101` does not apply, and neither does the GLSL hash trap at
`README.md:114` — the hash is integer `Math.imul` mixing, not `fract(sin(dot(...)))`.

Cost: one 30s composition at the default `AMBIENT_CANVAS_ROTATION_SECONDS`, so roughly 48MB of segment file
at the ~1.6MB per recorded second in `README.md:130`, and only its own ~30s of encode because the record
pass is incremental.

## Why it resonates
> A schematic prints itself out of nothing: a bright band of box-drawing characters at the top, structure
> hanging below it and dimming into a dot field, and a noise bed that swells with the density. It is one
> mechanism, visible in full, and the sound is not decoration but the same data read a second way.

## What to steal
- One rule, two senses. The grid is the score and the audio is the score played, so image and sound cannot
  drift apart. Most audio-reactive work runs the other direction and has to fake the coupling.
- Age as the only depth cue. There is no perspective and no colour; a row is further away because it is
  older, and brightness alone carries it.
- Structure beats noise. Long connected runs and rounded corners read as a circuit; the same character
  density scattered randomly reads as static. Connectivity is what makes glyphs legible as a drawing.
- A visible generation front. Pinning the bright band to one edge and letting everything else fall away
  turns a static texture into a process you can watch.

## Adopted
Rebuilt as a native ambient-canvas screensaver scene. The source is 1440x1680 portrait against a fixed
1920x1080 loop, and the loop is silent by design (the player hard-mutes), so the adopt keeps the plotter and
drops the sonification: a monochrome box-drawing schematic emitted at a bright front and accreting downward
over a dim glyph lattice, every mark hashed from its own integer coordinates so a frame is a pure function
of elapsed time, with 512 rows of pre-roll so the segment opens on a full field instead of an empty one.

`web/scenes/ascii-plotter/` in the dotfiles repo. The useful finding past this piece: a Canvas 2D scene
sidesteps the `preserveDrawingBuffer` trap entirely, and `Math.imul` integer mixing avoids the GLSL
`fract(sin(dot(...)))` moire that bit the-link, so a glyph scene is much cheaper to make deterministic than
a particle field.
