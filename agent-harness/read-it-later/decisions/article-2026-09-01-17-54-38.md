## What it actually is

A high-confidence compilation of behavioral-reading and persuasion claims, not a demonstrated psychology method. One
claim is directly contradicted by controlled evidence: Wiseman et al.'s three studies found no support for the
Neuro-Linguistic Programming claim that gaze direction distinguishes lies from truth
([PLOS ONE, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0040259)).

There is narrower evidence that a person's **own** head movement can affect attitude confidence, but it does not support
the reel's hand-mirroring mechanism or unconditional conclusion. Briñol and Petty induced participants to nod or shake
their heads while hearing a persuasive message; nodding increased persuasion for strong arguments, while the direction
reversed for weak arguments
([Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12793580/)). The reel removes
those conditions and turns a contingent laboratory effect into a covert-control recipe.

The source supplies no evidence for diagnosing suggestibility from lower-eyelid creases or for the absolute claim that
a self-generated inference cannot be resisted. At most, the juxtaposition segment illustrates a reason to keep supplied
facts separate from the conclusion a narrator invites the viewer to draw. That is a useful warning, but it is not a new
method and the reel itself repeatedly violates it.

compilation would preserve a misleading mnemonic more readily than a reusable insight.
